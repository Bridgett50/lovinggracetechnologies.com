What a custom web application should include

Custom software earns its keep when it fits one workflow exactly. Here is what to require before it is built.

1. The workflow, as it actually runs

Software built from a generic template fits generic work. A custom application starts from your workflow: the steps, the handoffs, the decisions, and the records. If the builder cannot describe your workflow back to you, the build will not fit it.

2. Approval gates

Anything the application sends, pays, or changes should pass a person first. Approval is a feature, not a policy document. In Second Seat, nothing is paid until the owner approves with one tap, and a separate checker verifies the draft first.

3. Evidence kept with the work

Every action should keep what it came from. A payment keeps its quote as its receipt. A plan keeps its reasons. An inventory record keeps its condition notes. When the evidence travels with the work, disputes get shorter and audits get easier.

4. Reasons you can read

When software scores, ranks, or recommends, it should explain why in plain words. Discovery Scout scores areas and shows the reasons for each score. A result without a reason is a guess wearing software.

5. People in charge of the edges

Software handles the routine. People handle the exceptions, the judgment calls, and the final say. The application should make those moments easy to find and easy to act on, not bury them.

That is the standard we build to: defined work, kept evidence, readable reasons, and a person approving what matters.

Describe the workflow you want software for. We will tell you plainly what it should include and what it should not.
