Gemini can make the call and handle checkout. The approval is still yours to give.

On Nov 17, 2026, Google made Tasks in Gemini free in the US, Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India. Gemini can call businesses, sign into a utility portal, compare prices, and check out with Google Pay, running on the new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model. Google says it is designed to pause and ask before it clicks Buy, and users can set a hard spending cap. The approval gate is the product.

On Nov 17, 2026, Google made Tasks in Gemini free. The announcement, "Put Gemini to work for you," was published on Google's Keyword blog by Josh Woodward, vice president of Labs, the Gemini app and AI Studio. Tasks is rolling out free of charge in the US, Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India, running on Google's new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model, with a set of advanced features starting in the US. The new free rollout follows an earlier paid offering. Tech Cheat Codes, citing reporting by Emma Roth at The Verge, identifies the previous product as Spark and reports that it was restricted to Google AI Pro or Ultra subscribers. Google's November 17 announcement does not itself establish that product history. The blog's subheading states the change plainly: "Gemini can now make the calls, pay the bills, and finish your tasks."

Business calling is one of the most consequential features for merchants and service providers, because it creates a direct interaction between a customer's AI assistant and a business. Woodward's example is a minivan with a check-engine light. He told Gemini the van was overdue for a service appointment and asked it to call the two nearest dealerships and find an early Friday morning slot. Gemini found the nearest shops on Google Maps and called both at once, sat through the hold music, found a time in his Google Calendar when he was free, and added the Friday early morning appointment in less than five minutes. On the dealership's end, Gemini disclosed right away that it is an AI. Businesses can opt out of these calls whenever they want, and Gemini will not give out the user's payment or private account information unless the user says so.

The money is the second thing, and Google built the controls into the announcement. A surprise $389 electric bill is Woodward's example. He gave Gemini permission to access the utility portal through the new Google Password Manager integration and asked it to analyze past billing statements in his email. Gemini found the heat pump kicking on around 4 p.m., in the middle of peak pricing. For shopping, he asked Gemini for the best options for a USB-C charger. Gemini compared chargers using Google's Shopping Graph, asked follow-up questions about wattage and color, helped him check out using the new Google Pay integration, and found a discount code that saved an extra $10.

Purchases run through the Agent Payments Protocol, known as AP2. Google says it is working with American Express (coming soon), Mastercard, and Visa to keep purchases secure with digital tokens on supported cards, and that shoppers still earn their card's points, cashback, or purchase protections during checkout. Users can set a hard spending cap upfront, and Gemini is designed to stay within the budget. Google also says Gemini is designed to request confirmation or pre-approval before clicking "Buy"; a spending limit does not, by itself, establish permission to make every purchase. Receipts and tracking information land in Google Wallet, which is expanding to iOS starting Nov 17. Next, Google says it will integrate the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) into Gemini for a faster shopping experience from discovery to checkout.

The reach goes beyond calls and shopping. Woodward texted Gemini through the new RCS integration to add groceries to an Instacart order, then sent a photo of an empty Cheerios box as a follow-up. Gemini added the items and handed him off to Instacart to complete the order. He forwarded a batch of school emails from Gmail to gemini@google.com, and in less than a minute every date was on his calendar with his wife invited. Gemini noticed a conflict during basketball practice and asked if he wanted to text another parent to trade carpool duty. Today, Gemini connects with more than 60 popular apps, and developers can apply to join Google's Connected Apps Platform. In the US, users can also start a task by texting Gemini after verifying a US phone number.

The controls are the part of the announcement that matters most for anyone who builds or buys agents. When Gemini is assigned a task, it uses Google Cloud's Agent Compute Engine to work in a hardware-isolated virtual machine dedicated to the user's account and never shared with other users. Each virtual machine is sandboxed, and the sandbox is one layer among several, including prompt-injection filters that scan search results and webpages and Safe Browsing limits on which links Gemini can open. As Gemini works, a user can jump in with more instructions at any point. It is designed to pause and check before critical steps. For example, Gemini is designed to ask for confirmation or pre-approval before clicking "Buy," and to ask clarifying questions if an instruction is ambiguous. For routine items the user already trusts, like adding groceries to a cart, the user can set that to "Always allow" so it does not ask each time. Users can adjust task settings, disconnect Google and third-party apps, or delete chat history at any time.

The limits are stated on the same page. Google says it has tested tasks extensively to minimize mistakes but has not eliminated all of them, and a task can fail to complete or miss the mark. On some sites, Gemini can make it most of the way through and then hand the screen back to the user to click the final button. The advanced features start in the US, and calling through Gemini is not available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, and Nebraska. The blog post itself carries a disclosure at the top: "This content is generated by Google AI. Generative AI is experimental".

Five checks worth keeping before letting an agent call, sign in, or pay on your behalf

  1. Set the spending cap before the first purchase. Google lets users set a hard spending cap upfront. Set it before Gemini shops, not after the first charge.
  2. Decide what "Always allow" covers. A routine grocery add is one thing. A purchase, a payment, or a sign-in is another. Keep "Always allow" narrow, and keep Buy on confirmation.
  3. Check the call rules for your state and your business. Calling is US only, and not in five states. If your business takes calls from customers, decide now whether to opt out of AI calls or to take them, and how your staff should handle one.
  4. Know what it can sign into, and with whose password. The Password Manager integration lets Gemini enter portals on the user's behalf. Review which logins are connected before handing it a task that touches money.
  5. Keep the receipt trail. Receipts and tracking land in Google Wallet, and the card's points and protections still apply. Check the Wallet record against the card statement, the same way you would check any charge.

What to watch

whether calling and payments extend beyond the US and beyond the six free countries; whether the Universal Commerce Protocol integration ships; whether the five-state calling exclusion changes; and whether businesses opt out of AI calls at scale or start answering them as a normal channel. The agent that pays is no longer a demo. It is free, in six countries, with a billion-plus user app behind it. The question for any business is not whether the agent will call. It is whether the business has decided what it may buy, and who approves it.
