Plan the hunt before you go

Discovery Scout helps people plan legal, permitted treasure-and-nature hunts. It uses openly licensed public data and a suitability score you can read and question. That score is planning help: it does not promise a find, and it is not a way to make money.

An explainable suitability score

The score draws on openly licensed public data and shows why a place scored the way it did. A higher score means the data looks more suitable for that kind of hunt. It does not mean you will find anything, and it does not replace permission, permits, or the rules for that land.

A U.S. dig-site directory

The directory points you to dig sites and to operators’ own websites. Loving Grace Technologies does not take the booking. You contact the dig site or tour operator and book with them directly.

Optional hunt plans

Done-for-you hunt plans are part of the product. Paid planning options are coming later. Nothing on this page is for sale, and Discovery Scout is not open for paid signups while it is in private testing.

Hunt types

What you can plan

Each plan starts from a kind of hunt, then checks whether that activity fits the land. You still confirm permission and the local rules before you go.

Gem and mineral collecting

Plan hobby collecting where it is allowed, then confirm the landowner or land agency and the site rules.

Gold panning

See where public data suggests panning may be suitable. The score is planning information, not a promise of gold.

Fossils

Plan fossil outings that follow the rules for that land. Some places allow hobby collecting, and some do not.

Shark teeth

Plan beach and site visits for shark-tooth hunting where collecting is allowed.

Meteorites

Meteorite collecting depends on who owns the land. On U.S. public land, follow the current rules of the agency that manages it. Some areas allow limited personal collecting, and some do not allow it at all.

Metal detecting of modern items

Modern items only, with the landowner’s permission and the local rules.

Storm and expedition trips

Plan the outing with the same care for permission and land status. If a dig site or operator is part of the trip, you book them directly.

Before you go

Permission, land status, and the rules

  • Get permission first. Private land needs the landowner’s yes. Public land needs you to know who manages it and what that agency allows.
  • Follow the operator’s rules and the law for that place. A directory listing is not permission to dig.
  • Use the suitability score as a planning aid. It is not a guarantee of a find, and it is not a way to earn money.
  • Metal detecting in Discovery Scout is for modern items only, with landowner permission and local rules.
  • For meteorites on U.S. public land, follow the land agency’s current rules, including any personal-use limits and any closures.
  • When a plan names a dig site or tour operator, you make the booking with them. Loving Grace Technologies does not book the trip.
