NVIDIA's $115.8 billion quarter: AI infrastructure revenue more than doubles year over year.

NVIDIA reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the third quarter of fiscal 2027, up 103 percent from a year earlier, with data center revenue of $107.6 billion. Vera Rubin shipped, the outlook for the fourth quarter is $130 billion, and the release launched an agent safety platform.

On Nov 17, 2026, NVIDIA reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the third quarter of fiscal 2027, the three months ended Oct 25, 2026. Revenue rose 103 percent from a year earlier and 20 percent from the previous quarter. Data center revenue, the segment that sells the systems AI labs and cloud companies run on, was $107.6 billion, up 110 percent from a year earlier and 21 percent from the previous quarter. GAAP earnings per diluted share were $2.70. Non-GAAP earnings per diluted share were $2.68. Gross margin was 74.8 percent on a GAAP basis and 74.9 percent on a non-GAAP basis. Net income was $65.3 billion on a GAAP basis, up 105 percent from a year earlier.

The results were NVIDIA's first quarterly earnings report since the company publicly announced Jensen Huang's resignation as chief executive on November 12, 2026. NVIDIA's SEC filing states that the board accepted his resignation on November 11, effective immediately. Colette Kress, NVIDIA's chief financial officer since 2013, is serving as interim chief executive while the board searches for a permanent successor. Kress and board chair Harvey C. Jones participated in the November 17 earnings call. During the November 17 earnings call, board chair Harvey Jones said the board did not intend to reinstate Huang as chief executive, according to contemporaneous reporting. In a November 17 report, The Wall Street Journal said the SEC was investigating NVIDIA following Huang's departure, including questions about whether disclosures to investors were misleading. NVIDIA's earnings release refers generally to government and regulatory inquiries but does not specifically identify an SEC investigation.

Vera Rubin is the hardware story. The Vera Rubin NVL72 shipped in the quarter, which NVIDIA calls the fastest product ramp in the company's history, with racks running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, and Nebius. Data center revenue of $107.6 billion is about 93 percent of total revenue.

The outlook is bigger and thinner. NVIDIA expects fourth-quarter revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3 percent, in a quarter with an additional week, and it is not assuming any data center compute revenue from China in that outlook. Gross margins are expected at 71.0 to 72.0 percent, down from 74.8 percent in the third quarter. On the November 17 earnings call, Colette Kress raised NVIDIA's expected fiscal 2028 year-over-year revenue growth to approximately 75 percent, from the approximately 70 percent outlook given in August. She also said the company expected gross margins to bottom in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027 before improving to approximately 72 to 73 percent in fiscal 2028.

The release also shows where the work is heading. NVIDIA launched the Open Agent Safety Platform and introduced the OpenShell Reference Architecture, which combines open source OpenShell software, Vera Rubin for model inference, Vera CPUs for agent execution, and BlueField-4 DPUs running NVIDIA Sentry to independently monitor agent behavior and enforce security policies in silicon. NVIDIA also announced an agreement to acquire Hugging Face, and expanded its collaboration with Meta on the AI agent Muse, with Meta planning expanded deployment of Vera Rubin, Vera CPUs, Spectrum-X networking, and BlueField-4 to scale training, inference, and agents. The buildout is no longer only about training bigger models. It is about running agents, and watching them while they run.

Five checks worth keeping when a quarter like this lands

  1. Demand doubling is not the same as your costs doubling. NVIDIA's data center revenue doubled because its customers are buying more compute. Your own AI bill follows your own usage, your own model choices, and your own approval gates, not NVIDIA's revenue line.
  2. Read the margin guide, not just the revenue beat. Revenue exceeded NVIDIA's previous $108.0 billion midpoint guidance by approximately $7.8 billion. Its fourth-quarter GAAP gross margin guidance of 71 to 72 percent is approximately 2.8 to 3.8 percentage points below the third quarter's reported 74.8 percent. A ramp costs money before it pays.
  3. Ask what the new platform actually ships. Vera Rubin NVL72 shipped, with racks running at named cloud providers. That is a shipped product, not a roadmap slide. The Open Agent Safety Platform is a launch; watch whether it becomes something teams actually deploy.
  4. Note what the outlook assumes away. NVIDIA assumes no data center compute revenue from China in the fourth quarter. Policy risk is inside the forecast, not outside it.
  5. Watch the governance story separately from the numbers. The first report under an interim chief executive, a board that says it does not intend to reinstate the founder, and a reported SEC investigation are a different set of facts from revenue growth. Both sets are real, and they answer different questions.

What to watch

whether the Vera Rubin ramp holds through the fourth quarter, whether gross margins bottom where Kress said they would, whether the 75 percent fiscal 2028 growth outlook survives contact with the year, and whether agent safety platforms like the one NVIDIA launched become a standard part of running agents, or stay a launch-day line. The news this week is that AI infrastructure revenue more than doubled year over year, and the work it enables is shifting from training models to running agents under watch.
