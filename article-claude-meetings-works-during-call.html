Research and Analysis

Claude joined the meeting. It started working before anyone hung up.

Anthropic published Claude Meetings on Nov 12, 2026. Claude joins a Google Meet call as a visible participant, answers questions, and builds docs and whiteboards while people are still talking. The limits matter as much as the feature: Meet only, Team and Enterprise only, and no outside guests by default.

On Nov 12, 2026, Anthropic published its announcement for Claude Meetings, now in beta. Claude can join a video call as a visible participant, follow the conversation, and start on work during the meeting instead of waiting until everyone hangs up. Anthropic's Nov 10 announcement listed Claude Meetings as coming soon; its own Meetings page, published Nov 12, put the beta in rollout. It is a meeting agent that works while people talk, not a recorder that summarizes afterward.

What Claude does in the call is specific. A team can ask it questions during the meeting, out loud or in chat. By default it answers in writing, and it can use connected tools with the person's permission. It builds docs, whiteboards, and other artifacts live, while teammates on the invite refine them. It writes the notes and action items, so no one on the call has to. After the call, the notes, action items, plan, and whiteboard sit on the meeting's page in claude.ai, and anyone who was part of the meeting can go back and ask Claude about it later.

Anthropic's own example makes the shape clear. A team planning a customer event tells Claude to start a doc for the plan, and Claude writes up what the team has said so far while teammates edit it live. When the budget comes up, someone asks what last year's event cost. Claude asks for approval to look it up, posts the answer in the meeting chat, and compares it to the current plan. When timelines come up, someone asks Claude to put the schedule on a whiteboard, and the team works on it alongside the doc. Before the meeting ends, someone asks Claude to share a recap with the team.

The limits are as important as the feature, and Anthropic states them plainly. Claude Meetings is in beta on Team and Enterprise plans only. Pro and Max subscribers and free users do not get it. At launch it works only with Google Meet, with other video conferencing platforms coming soon and no dates given. On Team plans, inviting Claude is on by default and an admin can turn it off. On Enterprise plans, an admin turns it on for all or part of the organization. During the beta it is not available to organizations with zero data retention, a HIPAA-ready configuration, or customer-managed encryption keys.

The outside-guest rule is the one to read twice. By default, Claude is set to stay out of calls when someone from outside the organization is invited, and to leave if someone from outside joins partway through. An admin can allow Claude on calls with outside guests. On those calls, Claude posts its notice in the call's chat but does not reply there, and what guests say is included in the notes and transcript. Claude uses each person's connected tools with that person's permission. It asks the first time, and a yes applies going forward. When someone asks out loud, Claude asks each time to confirm who is speaking. An answer posted in the call's chat is visible to everyone on the call.

The record Claude keeps is also defined. When Claude joins, it appears as a participant and posts a notice in the call's chat. It saves a transcript, summary, and screen descriptions, not audio. Anthropic's page carries a direct instruction: before adding Claude or turning on auto-join, make sure everyone on the calls is OK with being transcribed. Anthropic is also working with Gong, Attention, Granola, Fireflies.ai, Otter, and Plaud, so a meeting's transcript can soon be sent to the tools a team already uses.

The cost terms are time-limited and worth noting before any team builds on them. During the beta, Claude's notes and transcripts are free on Team and Enterprise plans. Its answers in calls are also free, unless an answer uses one of the person's connected tools, which counts toward the plan's usage. The docs, whiteboards, and other artifacts Claude makes in a call are free through Feb 10, 2027. Anthropic has published no price for the feature after the beta and no price for in-call artifacts after Feb 10, 2027, and no separate Meetings price at all.

Claude Meetings arrived in the same week Anthropic introduced Claude Sheets, Claude Whiteboards, and Claude Data in its Nov 10 announcement. The pattern is the same across all four: Claude moving from a chat window into the places where work already happens. Meetings is the one where the move is most visible, because the agent sits in the call, with a name on the participant list.

Five questions to ask before adding an AI participant to calls

  1. Does everyone on the call know it is being transcribed, and has each person agreed? Anthropic's own instruction is to make sure everyone is OK with being transcribed before turning auto-join on.
  2. What happens when an outside guest joins? The default keeps Claude out and removes it if a guest arrives partway. Any change to that default should be a deliberate decision, not an accident.
  3. What can it look up, and with whose permission? A budget lookup asks for approval first. Every connected tool should work the same way, with the permission belonging to the person who owns it.
  4. Where does the record live, and who can see it? Notes, transcript, and artifacts sit on a meeting page in claude.ai, visible to people from the organization on the invite. Know who else can open that page before the first call.
  5. What does it cost after the free period? Notes and transcripts are free during the beta, and artifacts are free through Feb 10, 2027. Nothing is published about the price after either date. Do not build a workflow on a price that does not exist yet.

What to watch

whether Zoom and Microsoft Teams support arrives and when, what Anthropic charges after the beta and after Feb 10, 2027, and whether the transcript partnerships with Gong, Fireflies.ai, Otter, and the others ship. A meeting agent that works during the call changes the meeting itself. The question for any team is not whether it can take notes. It is whether the team has decided, before the first call, who is in the room.
