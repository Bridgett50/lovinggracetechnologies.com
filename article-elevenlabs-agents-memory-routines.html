Voice agents that remember you. Half of it is a promise.

About half the package is preview or roadmap, not something you can use today.

ElevenLabs spent four years becoming the default name in AI voice. On Nov 11, 2026, at its first New York Summit, the company announced 13 new products and upgrades that turn it from a voice toolmaker into an enterprise agent platform. The package falls into four parts: custom models tuned on a customer's own data, voice agents that remember returning customers and automate follow-up work, a new workplace app for meetings, and a partner channel backed by NVIDIA infrastructure. The honest split, which the keynote does not draw for you, is that about half of it is something you can use now and about half is a preview with no date.

What is live now starts with ElevenAI, a new app that acts as an AI teammate. It listens to spoken conversations, including in-person meetings, and turns them into notes, summaries, and action items for each attendee. It also does dictation into any app and gives live assistance during a call. Free early access opened with the announcement on Nov 11. ElevenLabs built notice and consent mechanisms into the app rather than adding them later, which matters because an app that listens to in-person meetings carries a legal profile that varies by place, and the compliance obligation stays with the user.

Two models shipped the day before the Summit, on Nov 10. Scribe v3 is a speech-to-text transcription model covering 102 languages, which ElevenLabs says produces 27 percent fewer word errors than Scribe v2 on its own evaluation. Scribe v3 Live is the real-time version. It supports diarization for the first time, telling speakers apart in a live conversation, and ElevenLabs says it makes 43 percent fewer errors than its predecessor, with turn detection that reads the whole conversation rather than the last moment of audio. Both models are in the ElevenLabs API, Scribe v3 Live is in ElevenAgents, and Scribe v3 is now the default in the ElevenLabs app. A healthcare-tuned variant, Scribe v3 Medical, recognizes clinical terms. A third model family also arrived around the Summit: Eleven v4 for expressive text-to-speech, and Eleven Realtime, a speech-to-speech model built for fast voice agents.

Campaign Manager shipped Nov 9, two days before the Summit, and it is quietly the most commercially interesting launch of the three days. You give it contacts and a goal, a qualified lead or a confirmed appointment, and it works the follow-up over time. After each step it decides what happens next. No answer means try again later. A caller saying "call me back Thursday at 4pm" means it calls back then. You set the days and hours it may contact people and cap the attempts per person, and if someone asks not to be contacted it stops and logs it. Every campaign shows the full funnel from enrollment to outcome. It runs on phone calls and WhatsApp today, with email and SMS channels to come.

What is still a promise starts with the Interaction Engine, the centerpiece of the Summit. It is a system that manages a whole voice conversation, deciding when to speak and when to listen, and which AI model should handle each moment. A cheaper model handles simple requests, a frontier model steps in for complex ones like refunds. If it works, it is a direct cost lever on every agent deployment. It was previewed, not released, and no date was given.

ElevenAgents, the company's platform for deploying voice and chat agents across sales, support, recruitment, and operations, will add Memory and Routines, plus Architect, a tool for creating and modifying agents through natural-language instructions. Memory will let an agent build a record of a returning authenticated customer over time, capturing the things that never had a CRM field: preferences, constraints, the fact that someone mentioned moving in March. ElevenLabs frames it so the CRM stays the system of record and Memory holds the conversational residue. Routines add multi-step automation for the work around a conversation rather than the conversation itself: pre-screening a mortgage applicant and calling back within minutes, reminding patients before a visit, offering a cancelled slot to a waitlist, writing to a CRM afterwards. Neither has a ship date.

The fourth part is model customization. In the coming months, selected enterprises will work with ElevenLabs researchers to fine-tune open-source language models and ElevenLabs voice models on data from their own live deployments, picking their own target metrics. The company's chief executive, Mati Staniszewski, compared it to each company having its own reinforcement-learning stack, and said conversational agents will become critical infrastructure for every enterprise, as essential as cloud or payments, within a few years. Pricing is undisclosed, and the work starts with selected enterprises first, so whether mid-sized companies can afford it is an open question.

The NVIDIA deal ties the package to the hardware story. NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open vision-language model built for reasoning in agentic workloads, is now available inside ElevenAgents, and ElevenLabs plans to add 15 megawatts of NVIDIA Vera Rubin compute capacity on top of its existing Blackwell Ultra systems. ElevenLabs says Nemotron completed customer-support benchmark tasks at 70 percent lower cost than other leading open models. That is the company's own benchmark, not independently verified, so treat the number as a claim rather than a result.

A Partner Program and Marketplace round out the announcement, covering more than 550 partners in 44 countries.

Five questions worth keeping before buying into any of it:

  1. Which parts are live today, and which are previews? ElevenAI, Scribe v3, Scribe v3 Live, and Campaign Manager are usable now. Interaction Engine, Memory, Routines, and fine-tuning are not, and none has a date.
  2. What does free early access convert into? ElevenAI is free right now, and ElevenLabs has not published pricing for what comes afterwards, or what happens to your notes if you later stop paying.
  3. Where does the memory live, and who controls it? An agent that remembers a customer is holding personal history. The CRM stays the system of record in ElevenLabs' framing, but the consent, retention, and deletion terms for Memory have not been published with the preview.
  4. Whose benchmark is the savings number? The 70 percent cost figure for Nemotron and the 43 percent error reduction for Scribe v3 Live are ElevenLabs' own measurements. No independent comparison was published with either.
  5. Who can actually buy the custom-model work? Selected enterprises first, starting in the coming months, with no price. A small team should not plan around it until it opens wider.

What to watch

Whether Interaction Engine, Memory, and Routines get dates and reach general availability, whether the fine-tuning program opens beyond selected enterprises, and what ElevenAI costs once early access closes.
