Two big vendors, one agent platform. The 6,000 number needs reading.

The release names no price, no availability date, and no customer running it in production.

On Nov 10, 2026, IBM and Microsoft announced Imagine Enterprise, an agentic platform designed to help enterprises build, run, and govern AI agents that help people execute industry-specific processes. The announcement came from Armonk, New York, and Redmond, Washington, and it is a joint offer, not a product from one vendor. IBM Consulting supplies the agents, the industry knowledge, and its delivery platform. Microsoft supplies the software underneath.

The division of labor is the clearest part of the release. IBM Consulting brings more than 6,000 prebuilt AI agents and assets, built on IBM Consulting Advantage, its AI-powered delivery platform, plus industry expertise from building and running them. Microsoft provides the AI, data, cloud, and security foundation: Microsoft Foundry, GitHub Copilot, and Copilot Studio to coordinate agents with enterprise systems, and Microsoft Fabric, Microsoft 365, Entra, Purview, Defender, and Azure for data, identity, governance, security, and cloud. IBM Consulting Advantage contributes Process Studio, Context Studio, and Advantage Core to design, deploy, and govern tailored agents. Both companies offer forward-deployed engineering support for qualifying engagements, to move customers from experimentation to production.

The release frames the platform around three points. First, instant expertise: start with prebuilt agents and assets for your industry instead of building from nothing. Second, it fits the enterprise: connect to existing data, processes, systems, and technology, with security, identity, governance, and control built in. Third, it puts the knowledge of the business behind people and agents, so enterprise context travels with the work. The platform is built, in the release's words, to help organizations govern AI agents with controls, observability, and AI-compliance support, aimed especially at regulated industries.

The number in the headline needs reading twice. The release says more than 6,000 AI agents and assets. It does not say how many of the 6,000 are deployable agents and how many are other assets, such as templates, accelerators, or reusable components. A buyer who reads it as 6,000 ready agents will be wrong. The release also names no price, no general availability date, and no customer running the platform in production. The one named customer, SEI, a financial technology and asset management firm, is described as exploring emerging and agentic technologies with IBM Consulting and Microsoft, not as running Imagine Enterprise live. SEI manages, advises, or administers about $2 trillion in assets, as of Sept 30, 2026, per the release.

The first industry frameworks cover financial services, insurance, and retail. The release says they are designed to establish a repeatable blueprint for possible additions such as manufacturing, energy, the public sector, and mobility. Availability works through engagement: a client engages IBM Consulting to deploy and operate the platform on its behalf, or co-innovates with IBM Consulting on enterprise-scale implementations. Self-management options are also available. The release ends with the standard caution that statements about future direction are goals and objectives only, subject to change or withdrawal without notice.

What to watch

Whether IBM and Microsoft name a customer running the platform in production, whether they publish pricing or an availability date, and whether the public sector framework arrives, since that is the lane closest to government buyers. The announcement shows the enterprise agent market moving from selling models to selling governed platforms with engineers attached. The evidence for the platform itself is still the announcement.
