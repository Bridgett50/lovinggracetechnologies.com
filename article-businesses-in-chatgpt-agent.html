Research and Analysis

Your business can answer inside ChatGPT now. The agent is yours to check.

OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on Nov 10, 2026. Business Hub lets an eligible business claim its profile and see aggregated mentions and clicks. Eligible US ecommerce and service businesses can publish a Business Agent that answers customers inside ChatGPT, in a labeled conversation separate from ChatGPT's own answers.

On Nov 10, 2026, OpenAI introduced Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. Through Business Hub, businesses can claim their business, manage their information, and see insights on how people engage with them on ChatGPT. In addition, OpenAI added Business Agents, starting with eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the United States. As a first step, in beta, OpenAI is introducing Businesses in ChatGPT to connect the 1.2 billion people who use ChatGPT each week with businesses of all sizes, and to test and expand to more markets and business categories over time.

The claim is the foundation. In Business Hub, companies claim their organization and, once verified, update key details and add self-service product feeds with pricing and availability. Any eligible business located where ChatGPT is available can claim. Enterprise customers and advertisers work through their OpenAI representative. Shopify merchants can connect their store from Business Hub or the Shopify admin, and eligible products from Shopify merchants are already integrated into ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog. The point of claiming is accuracy. ChatGPT already names businesses in its answers whether or not they have claimed anything, so the claim is a chance to correct the record. OpenAI states on its business page that claiming does not guarantee higher placement or sales, and that ChatGPT continues to make independent recommendations. OpenAI also states that claiming a business on ChatGPT does not affect the business's ranking in ChatGPT responses or search results.

The insights are totals, not chats. Business Hub provides aggregated insights into how people engage with a business in ChatGPT: the number of mentions and website clicks a business receives, and discovery topics, common themes across conversations where the business's website is cited. OpenAI says it does not share users' conversations or Business Agent chats with businesses, and insights appear only when there are enough conversations to meet thresholds. A business sees how it is represented. It does not see who asked, or what they said.

The agent is the new part. After claiming their business, eligible organizations can build, test, and launch a Business Agent, a new way to answer questions and help people explore their offerings. When a business appears in a model response, people can click to see the business's ChatGPT profile, and if a Business Agent is available, start a conversation with the business to explore its offerings and ask follow-up questions. OpenAI's example is someone comparing products, asking about the differences between two options and which features fit their needs, with the agent helping from the business's information. The Business Agent conversation is clearly labeled, separate from the customer's original ChatGPT conversation, and distinct from ChatGPT's independent answers. Businesses can also sponsor their Business Agent and use it as the destination for ChatGPT Ads, with sponsored placements clearly labeled and separate from ChatGPT responses. Business Agents are now available to eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the United States. This is an option for eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the US. It is not a statement that Business Agents are available to every business in those categories. The announcement alone does not establish the purchase or booking workflow, or that a business reviews the agent before publishing it. OpenAI's Help Center says Business Agents can answer questions based on a business's information, explain products and policies, and link back to the business's website for checkout or booking, and that before publishing a Business Agent, a business can test it and make changes.

That test before publishing is the control that matters. The agent answers from the business's information, in a labeled chat, and the business can test it and make changes before publishing. What the agent says is the business speaking, so the business should read what it says before customers do. The limits are part of the control too. The agent is limited to the information the business provides, and the conversation stays separate from ChatGPT's own answers, so a customer can tell which words came from the business and which came from ChatGPT. Claiming does not buy placement. It buys the chance to check the record and to check the agent.

Five checks worth keeping before a business claims its profile and publishes an agent

  1. Test the agent before it goes live. Before publishing, the business can test the agent and make changes. Test it with the questions customers actually ask, including the awkward ones.
  2. Feed it only information you stand behind. The agent answers from the business's information, so wrong prices, old hours, or discontinued products become the agent's answers.
  3. Keep the label visible. The agent conversation is labeled and separate from ChatGPT's own answers. If a customer cannot tell which is which, the setup is not ready.
  4. Know what you will see and what you will not. Insights are aggregated mentions and clicks, and only after thresholds are met. No individual conversations or agent chats are shared with the business. A blank metric can mean too little data, not zero activity.
  5. Read the terms before claiming. Claiming means accepting OpenAI's Businesses in ChatGPT Terms, last updated Nov 9, 2026, as reported in coverage that read them. The claim belongs to the owner or an authorized representative, and identity verification is part of the process.

What to watch

Whether Business Agents expand beyond US ecommerce and online services, and to which business categories; whether the agent gains abilities beyond answering questions; and whether claiming changes how often a business appears. OpenAI says claiming does not guarantee placement or sales, so the honest test is whether the agent answers customers well, not whether the listing ranks higher.
