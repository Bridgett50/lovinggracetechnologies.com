The Song

Before there was a march, there was a song. Toni Green's gospel blues testimony "God Knew Her Name" gives this gathering its name and message.

Read the story of the song and the Phoenix Tree below.

Our Purpose

For generations, women have carried the responsibilities of raising children, caring for families and keeping households together. Through widowhood, divorce, illness or the loss of a spouse's employment, many have also had to become breadwinners while continuing to care for their families. Men facing similar circumstances have taken on those same responsibilities, coming to understand from within what so many women have carried.

For those whose cries for recognition have gone unheard for years, this gathering is a place to be named and heard. God knew their names all along.

We gather in Washington to say what the song has said all along: no one who has prayed, served and carried pain faithfully has been forgotten.

Toni Green

International Queen of Blues

Toni Green is a Memphis-born soul and R&B vocalist. Her gospel blues testimony “God Knew Her Name” is the song this march carries to Washington. Memphis artist Toni Green’s song gives its name to this March on Washington.

“God Knew Her Name” was released in May 2026. It is a gospel blues testimony for the people who prayed, served and carried pain quietly, and were still seen by God: the mothers, grandmothers, church mothers, prayer warriors, caregivers, widows and quiet servants who kept showing up even when the world did not always see them.

The song's title says "Her," but its message was never only for women. It is for everyone who has ever felt unseen and kept going anyway: people overlooked, misnamed and forgotten by the world, who were never forgotten by God. God knew their name before the world ever mislabeled them.

As the song was being developed into a stage play, Toni envisioned herself as both a phoenix and a tree, with roots, a trunk and limbs, seeing in others what they could not always see in themselves. That vision became the Phoenix Tree concept. The original artwork, depicting a golden tree with phoenixes rising behind it, is the emblem of this march and the only logo used on this site.

The 30th Anniversary

On October 25, 1997, the Million Woman March took place in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. (Smithsonian historical collection record.)

Thirty years later, during the days leading to that anniversary, God Knew Her Name March on Washington will bring a new generation together for a gathering of its own.

We recognize the historical significance of this anniversary as we establish an event with its own name, purpose and identity.

We invite individuals, congregations and organizations nationwide to participate in shaping this gathering.

This gathering is not affiliated with the Million Woman March, the 1963 March on Washington, the King family, or the King Center.
