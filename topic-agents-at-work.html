AI agents doing real work: meetings, service, chat, calls, and voice. Eight articles, each with its own anchor link.

The AI teammate joined the meeting. Who approves what it learns?

xAI launched Grok Bot 1.0 on Nov 2, 2026. Team Bots now work in Slack, Microsoft Teams, and video calls, and they offer to take on work before anyone asks. The announcement does not say who approves what a shared Bot learns, or whose accounts it acts through. Those are the questions to ask before sharing one.

xAI says Grok Bot 1.0 is now available. Its announcement describes the product as more collaborative, proactive, and capable of completing work across team conversations, connected applications, and browser-based tasks. On Nov 2, 2026, xAI published the launch post for Grok Bot 1.0, and the post says it is available starting that day. 1.0's three main changes are that Bots can be shared with a team and brought into Slack, Microsoft Teams, and video calls; that Bots can proactively offer to take on work; and that Bots are faster and more reliable at completing complex, browser-based work, including writing documents and building presentations.

The shared part is the change that matters. A Team Bot can be shared with specific people or groups, and worked with in the app, in Slack, or in Microsoft Teams. In conversations it can follow discussions involving several people, understand who is involved, and contribute information or offer to pick up work. During a product launch, xAI's example has a Bot notice that new customer research points to a different message than the team is considering, bring that into the discussion, then coordinate with another Bot to update campaign materials once the team agrees on a direction. Bots can also join video calls and participate through the meeting chat, bringing information from earlier conversations into the discussion and keeping track of decisions. Team Bots are available on Team and Enterprise plans. Meetings are available on Team, Enterprise, and Ultra plans, and enterprise admins can turn meetings on in settings.

The second change is initiative. A Bot can monitor ongoing projects, recognize when something needs attention, and suggest next steps based on what it knows about the team's goals, processes, and priorities. It can follow up on unfinished work and bring people in when a decision requires their judgment. xAI's example is a launch Bot that notices an upcoming deadline and prepares the materials the team needs. xAI says Bots respect individual permissions, ask before sensitive actions, and check whether information is appropriate for public channels, and that Bots can now better distinguish actions that are already authorized from those requiring another request for permission.

The third change is memory, and it is the one that needs the closest look. As Bots work with different people, xAI says they learn more about the team, its goals and its work. The announcement also says a Bot can suggest changes to how it works based on what it has learned. The announcement also lists new Bot settings and analytics, which let teams manage what a Team Bot knows and see how the team uses it, plus interactive Pages that Bots can create and users can edit together, better sign-in handling, improved connections to Gmail, Google Calendar, and GitHub, and fewer resources needed to complete tasks.

What the announcement does not say is who approves what a Bot learns before the whole team inherits it, or whose connected accounts a shared Bot acts through. Those are the two questions the launch leaves open, and they are the ones that matter most for a team deciding whether to share a Bot. A personal assistant acts for one person. A team Bot acts for many people, in shared channels, and carries what it learned from one person into work for another. The safeguards xAI names are permissions, asking before sensitive actions, and checking what is appropriate to share in a public channel. The record that proves those safeguards worked is the approval log: what the Bot was taught, who approved it, and what it did. The announcement does not describe that log.

Five checks worth keeping before a shared Bot joins the team

  1. Share with specific people first. A Team Bot is shared with specific people or groups, not the whole company at once. Start narrow, and widen only when the team is comfortable.
  2. Ask who approves what the Bot learns. The announcement does not name an approval step for shared learning. Before sharing a Bot, ask how a lesson one person teaches reaches everyone else, and who can stop it.
  3. Ask whose accounts it acts through. The announcement does not say whose connected apps a shared Bot uses. Do not assume it is yours, or that it is everyone's. Get the answer before the Bot touches a shared system.
  4. Keep meeting participation visible, and check what it carries into public channels. A Bot that joins a call and takes part in the chat should be named in the invite, and the record should show what it brought into the discussion. xAI says Bots check whether information is appropriate to share in a public channel. Hold them to it.
  5. Treat proactive work as a proposal. A Bot that spots unfinished work and offers to take it on should show the work first. The team decides, then the Bot acts.

What to watch

Whether xAI publishes the learning-approval and credential answers, whether meeting participation grows beyond the chat, and whether other agent products publish their own shared-learning controls. The news this week is that the teammate is no longer personal. It belongs to the team now. The announcement does not yet say who approves what it learns, or whose accounts it acts through. Until it does, those are questions to ask before sharing a Bot.

Claude joined the meeting. It started working before anyone hung up.

Anthropic published Claude Meetings on Nov 12, 2026. Claude joins a Google Meet call as a visible participant, answers questions, and builds docs and whiteboards while people are still talking. The limits matter as much as the feature: Meet only, Team and Enterprise only, and no outside guests by default.

On Nov 12, 2026, Anthropic published its announcement for Claude Meetings, now in beta. Claude can join a video call as a visible participant, follow the conversation, and start on work during the meeting instead of waiting until everyone hangs up. Anthropic's Nov 10 announcement listed Claude Meetings as coming soon; its own Meetings page, published Nov 12, put the beta in rollout. It is a meeting agent that works while people talk, not a recorder that summarizes afterward.

What Claude does in the call is specific. A team can ask it questions during the meeting, out loud or in chat. By default it answers in writing, and it can use connected tools with the person's permission. It builds docs, whiteboards, and other artifacts live, while teammates on the invite refine them. It writes the notes and action items, so no one on the call has to. After the call, the notes, action items, plan, and whiteboard sit on the meeting's page in claude.ai, and anyone who was part of the meeting can go back and ask Claude about it later.

Anthropic's own example makes the shape clear. A team planning a customer event tells Claude to start a doc for the plan, and Claude writes up what the team has said so far while teammates edit it live. When the budget comes up, someone asks what last year's event cost. Claude asks for approval to look it up, posts the answer in the meeting chat, and compares it to the current plan. When timelines come up, someone asks Claude to put the schedule on a whiteboard, and the team works on it alongside the doc. Before the meeting ends, someone asks Claude to share a recap with the team.

The limits are as important as the feature, and Anthropic states them plainly. Claude Meetings is in beta on Team and Enterprise plans only. Pro and Max subscribers and free users do not get it. At launch it works only with Google Meet, with other video conferencing platforms coming soon and no dates given. On Team plans, inviting Claude is on by default and an admin can turn it off. On Enterprise plans, an admin turns it on for all or part of the organization. During the beta it is not available to organizations with zero data retention, a HIPAA-ready configuration, or customer-managed encryption keys.

The outside-guest rule is the one to read twice. By default, Claude is set to stay out of calls when someone from outside the organization is invited, and to leave if someone from outside joins partway through. An admin can allow Claude on calls with outside guests. On those calls, Claude posts its notice in the call's chat but does not reply there, and what guests say is included in the notes and transcript. Claude uses each person's connected tools with that person's permission. It asks the first time, and a yes applies going forward. When someone asks out loud, Claude asks each time to confirm who is speaking. An answer posted in the call's chat is visible to everyone on the call.

The record Claude keeps is also defined. When Claude joins, it appears as a participant and posts a notice in the call's chat. It saves a transcript, summary, and screen descriptions, not audio. Anthropic's page carries a direct instruction: before adding Claude or turning on auto-join, make sure everyone on the calls is OK with being transcribed. Anthropic is also working with Gong, Attention, Granola, Fireflies.ai, Otter, and Plaud, so a meeting's transcript can soon be sent to the tools a team already uses.

The cost terms are time-limited and worth noting before any team builds on them. During the beta, Claude's notes and transcripts are free on Team and Enterprise plans. Its answers in calls are also free, unless an answer uses one of the person's connected tools, which counts toward the plan's usage. The docs, whiteboards, and other artifacts Claude makes in a call are free through Feb 10, 2027. Anthropic has published no price for the feature after the beta and no price for in-call artifacts after Feb 10, 2027, and no separate Meetings price at all.

Claude Meetings arrived in the same week Anthropic introduced Claude Sheets, Claude Whiteboards, and Claude Data in its Nov 10 announcement. The pattern is the same across all four: Claude moving from a chat window into the places where work already happens. Meetings is the one where the move is most visible, because the agent sits in the call, with a name on the participant list.

Five questions to ask before adding an AI participant to calls

  1. Does everyone on the call know it is being transcribed, and has each person agreed? Anthropic's own instruction is to make sure everyone is OK with being transcribed before turning auto-join on.
  2. What happens when an outside guest joins? The default keeps Claude out and removes it if a guest arrives partway. Any change to that default should be a deliberate decision, not an accident.
  3. What can it look up, and with whose permission? A budget lookup asks for approval first. Every connected tool should work the same way, with the permission belonging to the person who owns it.
  4. Where does the record live, and who can see it? Notes, transcript, and artifacts sit on a meeting page in claude.ai, visible to people from the organization on the invite. Know who else can open that page before the first call.
  5. What does it cost after the free period? Notes and transcripts are free during the beta, and artifacts are free through Feb 10, 2027. Nothing is published about the price after either date. Do not build a workflow on a price that does not exist yet.

What to watch

whether Zoom and Microsoft Teams support arrives and when, what Anthropic charges after the beta and after Feb 10, 2027, and whether the transcript partnerships with Gong, Fireflies.ai, Otter, and the others ship. A meeting agent that works during the call changes the meeting itself. The question for any team is not whether it can take notes. It is whether the team has decided, before the first call, who is in the room.

The customer sent an agent. Service platforms answered in the same week.

Between Nov 10 and Nov 13, 2026, Zendesk, NiCE, and Intercom each shipped something for conversations where the customer is software. The question is no longer whether agents will call. It is whether service can tell an agent from a person, and govern it.

In four days in November, the customer changed shape. On Nov 10, 2026, Zendesk introduced its Outcome AI Platform at its AI Summit in San Francisco, built for a world where the first contact from a customer may come from that customer's own AI agent. On Nov 12, NiCE launched the next generation of NiCE Cognigy, designed so its agents can serve customers who arrive with a personal AI agent of their own. On Nov 13, Intercom began flagging whether a new web Messenger conversation was started by a personal agent. The three announcements address the same new fact from three sides: the caller may be software, and service has to decide what to do about it.

Zendesk's answer is the most complete of the three. Its Outcome AI Platform is powered by what Zendesk calls the Autonomous Service Workforce, and its centerpiece is Zendesk Bridge, a governed layer that brings Zendesk's specialized tools, context, and AI agents into the tools customers and employees already use. A support lead can investigate a payment issue in ChatGPT or Claude using a Zendesk ticket. A customer can get help from an AI agent inside a custom app. An employee can get an onboarding question answered in Microsoft Copilot. Zendesk's Bridge FAQ states the Bridge MCP tool catalog includes more than 400 Zendesk tools. Bridge is in early access. Administrators choose which agents can connect, who can use them, and what they can do. Existing user permissions still apply, and teams can see which agent acted and what happened. Every action passes through Bridge, which applies identity, permissions, policy, human approvals, observability, and audit.

The second Zendesk piece is a directory. The Open Service Directory is a public directory and open specification that helps a customer's personal AI assistant find a brand's verified support channel, whether or not the brand uses Zendesk. Brands approve their listings and control what information they share and when human review is required. The directory does not access private customer data and does not take service actions. It is listed as launching soon, not live. Early participants named in the release include Betterment, ElevenLabs, Flex, TeamSystem, TBA Auctions, Telekom Payment S.A., and Wireless Logic.

The third piece is how the work is priced. Zendesk Unlimited drops seat-based pricing for the new capabilities and charges by outcome instead. Customers set an Outcome Budget and pay for eligible issues resolved and business work completed. Zendesk says it evaluates each outcome for quality and completeness, so customers can see what counts against the budget. The release gives no per-outcome rates, no Outcome Budget minimums, and no availability dates for Zendesk Unlimited. Two kinds of outcomes count: Conversational Outcomes, when an issue is successfully addressed inside a customer interaction, and Operational AI Outcomes, when an automated workflow is completed across business systems. Zendesk's own survey, reported in the release, found 52 percent of consumers comfortable relying on personal AI assistants for everyday tasks, 63 percent saying companies will need to redesign customer service to accommodate them, and 84 percent saying human help should always be available.

Zendesk also published an early result for its employee-service agents. In their first two months of customer use, 300 customers executed 9 million workflows, work Zendesk estimates would otherwise have taken 750,000 hours to complete manually. That hours figure is Zendesk's estimate, not an audited measurement. Employee Service AI Agents coordinate steps across connected systems, so a request for software access or an employment letter can move through approvals and records in IT, HR, and Finance without the employee chasing each one. Industry Agents bring specialized knowledge to common service needs, and businesses can build Custom Agents for their own workflows. Self-Improving Zendesk, now generally available, connects Agentic Analytics, Admin Copilot, AI Agent Launchpad, and a Quality Score so teams can find patterns, test changes, and measure whether repeat contacts fall. People review and approve changes before they go live.

NiCE's Nov 12 launch takes a different angle on the same problem. The next generation of NiCE Cognigy builds agents from skills, tools, and personas that teams create once and reuse across agents. Agents coordinate specialized sub-agents for complex work and connect with external agents, including customers' personal AI agents, through open standards. The release does not name the standards. New in the release are AI Assist, which guides setup, testing, and improvement; tracing, which shows every step an agent takes, the data and tools it uses, and the decisions it makes; and native speech-to-speech voice. Default speech, text, and language models are included in the conversation price, with no separate model contract, and premium models from Deepgram, ElevenLabs, and Anthropic are available with usage-based billing. Existing customers get the next generation as part of their subscription, and production deployments keep running unchanged. NiCE published no prices and no performance figures in the release.

Intercom's Nov 13 change is the smallest and maybe the most practical. Every new web Messenger conversation now has a Personal agent involved attribute, set to Yes, Likely, or No, so a team can see which ones an agent probably started, find them in Inbox views, and search and route them. A new Personal agents report shows how Fin handles them, including resolution and escalation rates. Bain and Company published a brief on Nov 10, 2026, "When Your Customer Sends an Agent," telling service leaders to decide now which interactions to block, tolerate, or serve. The brief reports that, in Bain's discussions with customer experience leaders at major service organizations, no one could reliably identify what share of inbound contacts came from personal agents.

Read together, the week draws a line. The agent is no longer only the company's tool. It is also the customer's representative, arriving through channels the company does not control, with permissions the company did not grant. The platforms that moved first all reached for the same controls: a verified front door, permissions that follow the agent, a record of what it did, and a human who can still be reached.

Five checks worth keeping before any team lets outside agents into service

  1. The front door is verified. A personal assistant should reach the brand's official support channel through a directory the brand approved, not through a search result it guessed.
  2. Permissions travel with the agent. Which agents can connect, who can use them, and what they can do are set in advance, and existing user permissions still apply.
  3. Every action is logged with its actor. The record shows which agent acted, what it did, and what happened, including what was blocked and why.
  4. A human stays reachable. Zendesk's own survey puts it at 84 percent: human help should always be available. An agent-only path with no human exit is a design failure, not an efficiency gain.
  5. The price follows the outcome, and the outcome is defined in advance. If a vendor charges per resolution, get in writing what counts as one, who judges it, and what happens when the customer comes back.

What to watch

whether Zendesk Bridge leaves early access and the Open Service Directory goes live, whether the directory draws in brands that are not Zendesk customers, and whether other service platforms start labeling agent-started conversations the way Intercom now does. The customer sending an agent is no longer a prediction. It is a traffic type, and this week it got its first plumbing.

Your business can answer inside ChatGPT now. The agent is yours to check.

OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on Nov 10, 2026. Business Hub lets an eligible business claim its profile and see aggregated mentions and clicks. Eligible US ecommerce and service businesses can publish a Business Agent that answers customers inside ChatGPT, in a labeled conversation separate from ChatGPT's own answers.

On Nov 10, 2026, OpenAI introduced Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. Through Business Hub, businesses can claim their business, manage their information, and see insights on how people engage with them on ChatGPT. In addition, OpenAI added Business Agents, starting with eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the United States. As a first step, in beta, OpenAI is introducing Businesses in ChatGPT to connect the 1.2 billion people who use ChatGPT each week with businesses of all sizes, and to test and expand to more markets and business categories over time.

The claim is the foundation. In Business Hub, companies claim their organization and, once verified, update key details and add self-service product feeds with pricing and availability. Any eligible business located where ChatGPT is available can claim. Enterprise customers and advertisers work through their OpenAI representative. Shopify merchants can connect their store from Business Hub or the Shopify admin, and eligible products from Shopify merchants are already integrated into ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog. The point of claiming is accuracy. ChatGPT already names businesses in its answers whether or not they have claimed anything, so the claim is a chance to correct the record. OpenAI states on its business page that claiming does not guarantee higher placement or sales, and that ChatGPT continues to make independent recommendations. OpenAI also states that claiming a business on ChatGPT does not affect the business's ranking in ChatGPT responses or search results.

The insights are totals, not chats. Business Hub provides aggregated insights into how people engage with a business in ChatGPT: the number of mentions and website clicks a business receives, and discovery topics, common themes across conversations where the business's website is cited. OpenAI says it does not share users' conversations or Business Agent chats with businesses, and insights appear only when there are enough conversations to meet thresholds. A business sees how it is represented. It does not see who asked, or what they said.

The agent is the new part. After claiming their business, eligible organizations can build, test, and launch a Business Agent, a new way to answer questions and help people explore their offerings. When a business appears in a model response, people can click to see the business's ChatGPT profile, and if a Business Agent is available, start a conversation with the business to explore its offerings and ask follow-up questions. OpenAI's example is someone comparing products, asking about the differences between two options and which features fit their needs, with the agent helping from the business's information. The Business Agent conversation is clearly labeled, separate from the customer's original ChatGPT conversation, and distinct from ChatGPT's independent answers. Businesses can also sponsor their Business Agent and use it as the destination for ChatGPT Ads, with sponsored placements clearly labeled and separate from ChatGPT responses. Business Agents are now available to eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the United States. This is an option for eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the US. It is not a statement that Business Agents are available to every business in those categories. The announcement alone does not establish the purchase or booking workflow, or that a business reviews the agent before publishing it. OpenAI's Help Center says Business Agents can answer questions based on a business's information, explain products and policies, and link back to the business's website for checkout or booking, and that before publishing a Business Agent, a business can test it and make changes.

That test before publishing is the control that matters. The agent answers from the business's information, in a labeled chat, and the business can test it and make changes before publishing. What the agent says is the business speaking, so the business should read what it says before customers do. The limits are part of the control too. The agent is limited to the information the business provides, and the conversation stays separate from ChatGPT's own answers, so a customer can tell which words came from the business and which came from ChatGPT. Claiming does not buy placement. It buys the chance to check the record and to check the agent.

Five checks worth keeping before a business claims its profile and publishes an agent

  1. Test the agent before it goes live. Before publishing, the business can test the agent and make changes. Test it with the questions customers actually ask, including the awkward ones.
  2. Feed it only information you stand behind. The agent answers from the business's information, so wrong prices, old hours, or discontinued products become the agent's answers.
  3. Keep the label visible. The agent conversation is labeled and separate from ChatGPT's own answers. If a customer cannot tell which is which, the setup is not ready.
  4. Know what you will see and what you will not. Insights are aggregated mentions and clicks, and only after thresholds are met. No individual conversations or agent chats are shared with the business. A blank metric can mean too little data, not zero activity.
  5. Read the terms before claiming. Claiming means accepting OpenAI's Businesses in ChatGPT Terms, last updated Nov 9, 2026, as reported in coverage that read them. The claim belongs to the owner or an authorized representative, and identity verification is part of the process.

What to watch

Whether Business Agents expand beyond US ecommerce and online services, and to which business categories; whether the agent gains abilities beyond answering questions; and whether claiming changes how often a business appears. OpenAI says claiming does not guarantee placement or sales, so the honest test is whether the agent answers customers well, not whether the listing ranks higher.

Gemini can make the call and handle checkout. The approval is still yours to give.

On Nov 17, 2026, Google made Tasks in Gemini free in the US, Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India. Gemini can call businesses, sign into a utility portal, compare prices, and check out with Google Pay, running on the new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model. Google says it is designed to pause and ask before it clicks Buy, and users can set a hard spending cap. The approval gate is the product.

On Nov 17, 2026, Google made Tasks in Gemini free. The announcement, "Put Gemini to work for you," was published on Google's Keyword blog by Josh Woodward, vice president of Labs, the Gemini app and AI Studio. Tasks is rolling out free of charge in the US, Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India, running on Google's new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model, with a set of advanced features starting in the US. The new free rollout follows an earlier paid offering. Tech Cheat Codes, citing reporting by Emma Roth at The Verge, identifies the previous product as Spark and reports that it was restricted to Google AI Pro or Ultra subscribers. Google's November 17 announcement does not itself establish that product history. The blog's subheading states the change plainly: "Gemini can now make the calls, pay the bills, and finish your tasks."

Business calling is one of the most consequential features for merchants and service providers, because it creates a direct interaction between a customer's AI assistant and a business. Woodward's example is a minivan with a check-engine light. He told Gemini the van was overdue for a service appointment and asked it to call the two nearest dealerships and find an early Friday morning slot. Gemini found the nearest shops on Google Maps and called both at once, sat through the hold music, found a time in his Google Calendar when he was free, and added the Friday early morning appointment in less than five minutes. On the dealership's end, Gemini disclosed right away that it is an AI. Businesses can opt out of these calls whenever they want, and Gemini will not give out the user's payment or private account information unless the user says so.

The money is the second thing, and Google built the controls into the announcement. A surprise $389 electric bill is Woodward's example. He gave Gemini permission to access the utility portal through the new Google Password Manager integration and asked it to analyze past billing statements in his email. Gemini found the heat pump kicking on around 4 p.m., in the middle of peak pricing. For shopping, he asked Gemini for the best options for a USB-C charger. Gemini compared chargers using Google's Shopping Graph, asked follow-up questions about wattage and color, helped him check out using the new Google Pay integration, and found a discount code that saved an extra $10.

Purchases run through the Agent Payments Protocol, known as AP2. Google says it is working with American Express (coming soon), Mastercard, and Visa to keep purchases secure with digital tokens on supported cards, and that shoppers still earn their card's points, cashback, or purchase protections during checkout. Users can set a hard spending cap upfront, and Gemini is designed to stay within the budget. Google also says Gemini is designed to request confirmation or pre-approval before clicking "Buy"; a spending limit does not, by itself, establish permission to make every purchase. Receipts and tracking information land in Google Wallet, which is expanding to iOS starting Nov 17. Next, Google says it will integrate the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) into Gemini for a faster shopping experience from discovery to checkout.

The reach goes beyond calls and shopping. Woodward texted Gemini through the new RCS integration to add groceries to an Instacart order, then sent a photo of an empty Cheerios box as a follow-up. Gemini added the items and handed him off to Instacart to complete the order. He forwarded a batch of school emails from Gmail to gemini@google.com, and in less than a minute every date was on his calendar with his wife invited. Gemini noticed a conflict during basketball practice and asked if he wanted to text another parent to trade carpool duty. Today, Gemini connects with more than 60 popular apps, and developers can apply to join Google's Connected Apps Platform. In the US, users can also start a task by texting Gemini after verifying a US phone number.

The controls are the part of the announcement that matters most for anyone who builds or buys agents. When Gemini is assigned a task, it uses Google Cloud's Agent Compute Engine to work in a hardware-isolated virtual machine dedicated to the user's account and never shared with other users. Each virtual machine is sandboxed, and the sandbox is one layer among several, including prompt-injection filters that scan search results and webpages and Safe Browsing limits on which links Gemini can open. As Gemini works, a user can jump in with more instructions at any point. It is designed to pause and check before critical steps. For example, Gemini is designed to ask for confirmation or pre-approval before clicking "Buy," and to ask clarifying questions if an instruction is ambiguous. For routine items the user already trusts, like adding groceries to a cart, the user can set that to "Always allow" so it does not ask each time. Users can adjust task settings, disconnect Google and third-party apps, or delete chat history at any time.

The limits are stated on the same page. Google says it has tested tasks extensively to minimize mistakes but has not eliminated all of them, and a task can fail to complete or miss the mark. On some sites, Gemini can make it most of the way through and then hand the screen back to the user to click the final button. The advanced features start in the US, and calling through Gemini is not available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, and Nebraska. The blog post itself carries a disclosure at the top: "This content is generated by Google AI. Generative AI is experimental".

Five checks worth keeping before letting an agent call, sign in, or pay on your behalf

  1. Set the spending cap before the first purchase. Google lets users set a hard spending cap upfront. Set it before Gemini shops, not after the first charge.
  2. Decide what "Always allow" covers. A routine grocery add is one thing. A purchase, a payment, or a sign-in is another. Keep "Always allow" narrow, and keep Buy on confirmation.
  3. Check the call rules for your state and your business. Calling is US only, and not in five states. If your business takes calls from customers, decide now whether to opt out of AI calls or to take them, and how your staff should handle one.
  4. Know what it can sign into, and with whose password. The Password Manager integration lets Gemini enter portals on the user's behalf. Review which logins are connected before handing it a task that touches money.
  5. Keep the receipt trail. Receipts and tracking land in Google Wallet, and the card's points and protections still apply. Check the Wallet record against the card statement, the same way you would check any charge.

What to watch

whether calling and payments extend beyond the US and beyond the six free countries; whether the Universal Commerce Protocol integration ships; whether the five-state calling exclusion changes; and whether businesses opt out of AI calls at scale or start answering them as a normal channel. The agent that pays is no longer a demo. It is free, in six countries, with a billion-plus user app behind it. The question for any business is not whether the agent will call. It is whether the business has decided what it may buy, and who approves it.

Voice agents that remember you. Half of it is a promise.

About half the package is preview or roadmap, not something you can use today.

ElevenLabs spent four years becoming the default name in AI voice. On Nov 11, 2026, at its first New York Summit, the company announced 13 new products and upgrades that turn it from a voice toolmaker into an enterprise agent platform. The package falls into four parts: custom models tuned on a customer's own data, voice agents that remember returning customers and automate follow-up work, a new workplace app for meetings, and a partner channel backed by NVIDIA infrastructure. The honest split, which the keynote does not draw for you, is that about half of it is something you can use now and about half is a preview with no date.

What is live now starts with ElevenAI, a new app that acts as an AI teammate. It listens to spoken conversations, including in-person meetings, and turns them into notes, summaries, and action items for each attendee. It also does dictation into any app and gives live assistance during a call. Free early access opened with the announcement on Nov 11. ElevenLabs built notice and consent mechanisms into the app rather than adding them later, which matters because an app that listens to in-person meetings carries a legal profile that varies by place, and the compliance obligation stays with the user.

Two models shipped the day before the Summit, on Nov 10. Scribe v3 is a speech-to-text transcription model covering 102 languages, which ElevenLabs says produces 27 percent fewer word errors than Scribe v2 on its own evaluation. Scribe v3 Live is the real-time version. It supports diarization for the first time, telling speakers apart in a live conversation, and ElevenLabs says it makes 43 percent fewer errors than its predecessor, with turn detection that reads the whole conversation rather than the last moment of audio. Both models are in the ElevenLabs API, Scribe v3 Live is in ElevenAgents, and Scribe v3 is now the default in the ElevenLabs app. A healthcare-tuned variant, Scribe v3 Medical, recognizes clinical terms. A third model family also arrived around the Summit: Eleven v4 for expressive text-to-speech, and Eleven Realtime, a speech-to-speech model built for fast voice agents.

Campaign Manager shipped Nov 9, two days before the Summit, and it is quietly the most commercially interesting launch of the three days. You give it contacts and a goal, a qualified lead or a confirmed appointment, and it works the follow-up over time. After each step it decides what happens next. No answer means try again later. A caller saying "call me back Thursday at 4pm" means it calls back then. You set the days and hours it may contact people and cap the attempts per person, and if someone asks not to be contacted it stops and logs it. Every campaign shows the full funnel from enrollment to outcome. It runs on phone calls and WhatsApp today, with email and SMS channels to come.

What is still a promise starts with the Interaction Engine, the centerpiece of the Summit. It is a system that manages a whole voice conversation, deciding when to speak and when to listen, and which AI model should handle each moment. A cheaper model handles simple requests, a frontier model steps in for complex ones like refunds. If it works, it is a direct cost lever on every agent deployment. It was previewed, not released, and no date was given.

ElevenAgents, the company's platform for deploying voice and chat agents across sales, support, recruitment, and operations, will add Memory and Routines, plus Architect, a tool for creating and modifying agents through natural-language instructions. Memory will let an agent build a record of a returning authenticated customer over time, capturing the things that never had a CRM field: preferences, constraints, the fact that someone mentioned moving in March. ElevenLabs frames it so the CRM stays the system of record and Memory holds the conversational residue. Routines add multi-step automation for the work around a conversation rather than the conversation itself: pre-screening a mortgage applicant and calling back within minutes, reminding patients before a visit, offering a cancelled slot to a waitlist, writing to a CRM afterwards. Neither has a ship date.

The fourth part is model customization. In the coming months, selected enterprises will work with ElevenLabs researchers to fine-tune open-source language models and ElevenLabs voice models on data from their own live deployments, picking their own target metrics. The company's chief executive, Mati Staniszewski, compared it to each company having its own reinforcement-learning stack, and said conversational agents will become critical infrastructure for every enterprise, as essential as cloud or payments, within a few years. Pricing is undisclosed, and the work starts with selected enterprises first, so whether mid-sized companies can afford it is an open question.

The NVIDIA deal ties the package to the hardware story. NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open vision-language model built for reasoning in agentic workloads, is now available inside ElevenAgents, and ElevenLabs plans to add 15 megawatts of NVIDIA Vera Rubin compute capacity on top of its existing Blackwell Ultra systems. ElevenLabs says Nemotron completed customer-support benchmark tasks at 70 percent lower cost than other leading open models. That is the company's own benchmark, not independently verified, so treat the number as a claim rather than a result.

A Partner Program and Marketplace round out the announcement, covering more than 550 partners in 44 countries.

Five questions worth keeping before buying into any of it:

  1. Which parts are live today, and which are previews? ElevenAI, Scribe v3, Scribe v3 Live, and Campaign Manager are usable now. Interaction Engine, Memory, Routines, and fine-tuning are not, and none has a date.
  2. What does free early access convert into? ElevenAI is free right now, and ElevenLabs has not published pricing for what comes afterwards, or what happens to your notes if you later stop paying.
  3. Where does the memory live, and who controls it? An agent that remembers a customer is holding personal history. The CRM stays the system of record in ElevenLabs' framing, but the consent, retention, and deletion terms for Memory have not been published with the preview.
  4. Whose benchmark is the savings number? The 70 percent cost figure for Nemotron and the 43 percent error reduction for Scribe v3 Live are ElevenLabs' own measurements. No independent comparison was published with either.
  5. Who can actually buy the custom-model work? Selected enterprises first, starting in the coming months, with no price. A small team should not plan around it until it opens wider.

What to watch

Whether Interaction Engine, Memory, and Routines get dates and reach general availability, whether the fine-tuning program opens beyond selected enterprises, and what ElevenAI costs once early access closes.

Genesys let the AI agent in without moving the contact center.

On Nov 17, 2026, Genesys made its Agentic Virtual Agent available as a standalone product that runs with the contact center, phone numbers, and carrier a company already uses, plus Genesys Arena with prebuilt Agent Kits and effort-based pricing measured in Experience Units.

On Nov 17, 2026, Genesys introduced Genesys Standalone AI Agents. The change is about the starting point, not the agent itself. Until now, using the Genesys Agentic Virtual Agent meant moving the broader customer service environment to Genesys Cloud first. The standalone version drops that requirement. An organization can deploy the agent alongside the contact center it already runs, keeping its current human teams, phone numbers, carrier, and contact center investments in place. Genesys's own framing is plain: the first decision no longer has to be about infrastructure. It can be about the customer need worth solving first, such as changing an account, servicing a payment, progressing a claim, scheduling an appointment, or handling another high-volume request where the customer expects an outcome, not simply an answer.

From conversation to completed work

Genesys AI agents are powered by what the company calls Large Action Models, AI models designed not only to understand what a customer is asking, but to reason through the work required and act. An agent can determine what needs to happen next and use approved tools across enterprise systems to get the job done: finding information, updating a record, making a change, scheduling an appointment, or completing a multistep task across several systems. The shift Genesys names is from AI that can respond to AI that can act. That action happens within the controls the business sets. Approved tools, permissions, policies, and guardrails define what the AI can do. When human judgment is needed, the interaction can move to an employee with relevant context intact, so the customer does not have to start again. Genesys states the goal directly: not maximum autonomy, but useful, governed autonomy, enough independence to resolve more customer needs while keeping the business in control.

Arena: a working agent in as little as 10 minutes

For teams still deciding where to begin, Genesys Arena offers a faster starting point. It holds working AI agent prototypes, Agent Kits, voice personas, and workflows that let teams explore proven industry scenarios and adapt them to their own brand, systems, and processes. Genesys says organizations can create a functional AI agent in as little as 10 minutes, then test and deploy it. The Agent Kits cover more than 50 common industry use cases, and each kit supplies a working model with knowledge, skills, integrations, and guardrails. Standalone AI Agents were generally available starting Nov 17, 2026, while the Agent Kits will be generally available through Genesys Arena later in November 2026. Voice customization tools are coming to Arena and Genesys Cloud.

Effort-based pricing: pay for the work, not the talk

Genesys also introduced effort-based pricing, which prices each interaction by the work required to complete it, measured in tool calls. Simple interactions use 1 to 3 tool calls. Medium interactions use 4 to 6. Complex interactions use 7 or more. Consumption is measured in Genesys Cloud Experience Units, the new name for what Genesys previously called AI Tokens. Each interaction is counted once and assigned a pricing tier based on the tools required to complete it, so a simple, high-volume interaction costs less than one requiring the agent to perform more actions across more systems. The November 17 announcements did not specify numerical per-tier rates, so the actual cost of a simple, medium, or complex interaction is not stated in them. The pricing change reflects the same shift as the product: when AI primarily answered questions, measuring the interaction made sense. When AI starts doing real work, the work itself becomes the unit of value.

The results, and what they actually prove

Genesys reports results from three customers using its agentic AI. Electrolux Group has more than 65 percent of service demand supported by agentic AI, with 100 percent containment in select journeys. Riachuelo reached 84 percent self-service resolution compared with 30 percent using its previous traditional chatbot, alongside a 60 percent increase in customer satisfaction. Trademark Global reports a 111 percent increase in referral-code usage, 42 percent containment, and a 91 percent success rate for warranty claims. The caveat matters and Genesys states it in its own post: these results come from Agentic Virtual Agent deployments in broader Genesys environments, and they demonstrate the underlying agentic AI capabilities that power Standalone AI Agents. They are not results from standalone deployments. The standalone product was generally available starting Nov 17, 2026, so standalone results do not exist yet.

Measure whether the work got done

Genesys also argues the performance measure should change with the product. Containment still matters, but keeping a customer inside an automated interaction does not necessarily mean their problem was solved. The questions Genesys lists are: did the AI understand what the customer needed, did the right tools work, was the task completed, where did the AI need help, and did the customer reach the intended outcome? Genesys gives teams visibility into resolution and escalation, along with insight into why interactions succeed or fail. The point is the same one running through the whole announcement. The value of an AI agent is not that it can hold a convincing conversation. It helps the customer accomplish something, inside controls the business sets, with a human able to take over with context intact.

What to watch

Whether standalone deployments match the results Genesys reports from its full-platform deployments, now that the product is generally available. Whether Agent Kits reach general availability later in November as announced, and whether the 10-minute starting claim holds up on real customer systems. Whether Genesys publishes per-tier rates for effort-based pricing, because without them the model cannot be compared against per-token or per-seat pricing. And whether other contact center vendors follow Genesys in unbundling their agents from their platforms, because the decision Genesys removed, migrate first or wait, was the same decision keeping many teams from starting at all.

Databricks turns Genie into an always-on AI coworker. The always-on part is not generally available yet.

On Nov 11, 2026, Databricks announced a second-generation Genie that works autonomously on long-running enterprise work, grounded in Genie Ontology and governed by Unity Gateway. Genie and Genie Ontology are generally available; the new always-on mode is headed to preview and beta programs.

On Nov 11, 2026, Databricks announced the second generation of Genie, its enterprise AI agent, now positioned as an always-on, autonomous AI coworker. The pitch is a change of job description. Earlier Genie answered questions and ran analyses when asked. The new Genie takes a goal and owns the work: it delegates to subagents when needed, tracks progress, follows up on missing inputs, and reaches out when a person needs to make a decision. A new priority inbox collects what needs attention, and Genie sends an email alert when the user is away. Databricks says thousands of customers already use the established Genie product in production, including Zillow, Unilever, adidas, Atlassian, and AstraZeneca. That count covers the Genie product as it already runs, not adoption of the newly announced autonomous version. The availability line matters and Databricks states it plainly in the announcement: as of the Nov 11 announcement, Genie and Genie Ontology are generally available to all customers, while Genie Search, Ontology Manager, and the new always-on Genie are coming soon to preview and beta programs, with no exact preview or beta dates stated. The always-on mode is announced, not shipped to everyone.

What it does on its own

The work Genie takes on is the long-running kind that usually sits on a person's list. It can run on a schedule or respond to triggers, such as a metric dipping or more than ten customers asking for the same feature. Databricks illustrates this with three examples, presented as scenarios rather than documented deployments. In insurance, Genie can monitor incoming claims for emerging fraud patterns and loss-ratio drift, then assemble the case file and route it to underwriters when a segment moves outside guidelines. In marketing, it can manage campaigns around the clock, investigate underperformance, and propose weekly budget changes for approval. For an individual, it can monitor email and Slack, prepare meeting briefs, and alert the user as new things come up. It notices work a person repeats, like a weekly update, and offers to take it on. It can work on many jobs at once, and the user supervises, steers, and stops them from one control panel, deciding what Genie does on its own and what needs sign-off, within company guardrails. Databricks chief executive Ali Ghodsi put the claim personally: "Genie has made me a 10x more productive CEO, and now it doesn't wait for me to ask." That is the company's own statement about its own chief executive's use, not an independent result.

The context layer: Genie Ontology

The autonomy rests on Genie Ontology, which Databricks calls an agentic ontology: a shared map of how a company operates, covering definitions, metrics, relationships, and organizational knowledge. Agents build most of it automatically from the dashboards, queries, and notebooks people already use, and it keeps learning with every query and AI interaction. People govern it: teams establish authoritative definitions in Unity Catalog, and the OntoRank algorithm gives those definitions precedence, so every agent works from the same governed understanding. Organizational directories, starting with Workday, bring people, teams, and reporting relationships into the ontology, and OntoRank combines those relationships with activity signals showing who actually works together. An Ontology Manager gives teams one place to manage and customize context, with proactive gap alerts flagging concepts people ask about often that lack a governed definition. The point of the layer is that an agent that knows what counts as an active customer and which revenue metric is authoritative can act without asking about definitions every time. The risk it carries is the same as any shared definition store: if the governed definitions are wrong, every agent inherits the error, and the announcement gives no detail on how a wrong definition is caught or rolled back.

The search layer: Genie Search

Genie Search is the second new piece, a hybrid search that brings enterprise search into Databricks with no separate search infrastructure to procure and no separate permission model to maintain. It combines an index of content from business apps such as Google Drive, Jira, Confluence, Workday, and SharePoint with live search through MCP, the protocol for reaching tools and data outside the index. Databricks offers more than 100 connectors, spanning ingestion, indexing, and federated MCP access, so the count covers several connection methods rather than one method for every source. For each task, Genie draws from the index, MCP, or SQL, then composes one answer with citations. Through Unity Catalog, Genie Search maps each source's access controls alongside its content and enforces them before returning results, so nobody sees a document in Genie that they could not open in the original application. Genie Search also feeds Genie Ontology, so tickets and documents become evidence OntoRank weighs when deciding which sources to trust. Like the always-on mode, Genie Search is coming soon to preview and beta programs, not generally available.

The control layer: Unity Gateway

Unity Gateway is the admin control plane, and it is where the approval story lives. Genie acts with the user's identity and accesses only information that person is authorized to see, respecting Unity Catalog permissions and, for indexed content, each source system's permissions. Admins control connector access, MCP tools, and approval requirements, while business users choose what Genie can do autonomously within those limits. Every action Genie takes is traced and auditable. Admins can set workspace and per-user budgets, spending caps, and alerts, and can ask Genie to investigate usage and agent traces for wasted tokens and costly tool calls. When users need additional access, Genie can prepare an access request for them to review and send, while admins and data owners retain approval authority. Genie Agents now work across PDFs, contracts, and slides in Unity Catalog volumes alongside governed tables, with no separate vector index, and connect through MCP to Slack, Atlassian, GitHub, Gmail, Google Drive, and Microsoft 365. OpenSharing lets teams share governed agents with partners and customers, and Genie Clean Rooms lets recipients ask questions without exposing the underlying data or proprietary logic. Genie plugins bring the same business context into ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot Cowork, and Grok Bot. Genie is part of the Databricks Data and AI Platform, and customers pay for what they use, not per seat, with spending limits set by team or by user through Unity Gateway. No per-seat price and no usage rate are published in the announcement.

The benchmark number, read carefully

Databricks reports that in a new benchmark built with Turing, Genie, powered by Genie Ontology, answered correctly in 91 percent of task runs, 1.6 times the rate of the stronger of two leading frontier agents on the same tasks in the same Databricks workspace, while using less than half as many tokens. The tasks required connecting knowledge spread across tables, dashboards, and notebooks, not simply accessing data. Read it as stated. It is a vendor-run benchmark, the two frontier agents are unnamed, and the comparison pits Genie with its ontology against agents without it, so the 91 percent figure measures the value of the context layer as much as the agent itself. It does not establish how Genie performs in a new deployment with no accumulated business knowledge, or outside a Databricks workspace. Databricks says the new Genie can help with daily tasks and bigger responsibilities alike, from staying on top of email to keeping a quarterly forecast on track, but the always-on mode that does that work is still headed to preview and beta.

What to watch

When the always-on mode and Genie Search move from preview and beta to general availability, and who gets in first. Whether the 91 percent benchmark result holds for customers whose ontology is thin or new, since the test ran in an established workspace. Whether admins use the Unity Gateway budgets and approval rules in practice, or whether the autonomy defaults stay wide open. And whether the ontology model spreads: if the shared business context is what makes an agent trustworthy enough to own long-running work, the next question is who writes the definitions, who fixes them when they are wrong, and how a small team without a data platform gets the same effect.
