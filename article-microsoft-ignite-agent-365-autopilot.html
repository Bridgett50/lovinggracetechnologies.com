Microsoft's Ignite answer: govern the agent, measure it, and give it an identity.

At Microsoft Ignite 2026 on Nov 17, Microsoft framed Copilot as the new operating system for work, put Autopilot in Frontier rollout as a long-running agent with its own identity and memory, and added Agent 365 cost management and business analytics. The governance arrived at the same time as the agents, and most of the new pieces are still in preview or the Frontier program.

Microsoft Ignite 2026 opened in San Francisco on Nov 17, 2026, and runs through Nov 20. Chairman and CEO Satya Nadella opened with a keynote framed around what Microsoft calls a frontier ecosystem: people and agents working together, organizations keeping control of their own intelligence, and trust in the technology. His three pillars were human ambition, intelligence, and trust. The announcements below are the ones that change how an organization runs agents, and the availability label on each one matters as much as the feature.

Microsoft describes Copilot as the new operating system for work, positioning it as a central place for conversations, delegated tasks, document creation, and collaboration. The new Copilot brings together Home, which is rolling out worldwide as the starting point for Chat and Cowork; Auto, which can recommend moving complex tasks into Cowork and includes additional model-routing capabilities for Frontier customers; and Office in Copilot, now generally available, which brings Word, Excel, and PowerPoint into conversations for document creation and collaboration. Files are saved to OneDrive and inherit Microsoft 365 permissions, sensitivity labels, and compliance controls. Copilot Code lets users build dashboards, trackers, and apps using natural language. Cloud projects run in a secure sandbox within the organization's tenant, while local sandbox protections are available on Windows and planned for macOS. Copilot Code is available through the Frontier program on Windows, web, iOS, and Android, with general availability planned for December 2026. Copilot Workspaces gives teams, their work, and Copilot a shared space in the Copilot app, and it is available through the Frontier program. Copilot Managed Runtime, the Microsoft-managed hosting and execution layer for what people build in Copilot, is now generally available, with an optional Fabric data backend.

Autopilot is the announcement to read twice. Microsoft calls it its first enterprise-grade, long-running agent for personal productivity. It lives in an organization's tenant with its own identity, memory, and access to tools. It can follow up on threads, run recurring work, and continue a project without waiting for another prompt. Colleagues will be able to @mention a person's Autopilot in a Teams channel or group chat to ask for information or request supported actions. The security design is stated in the Book of News: actions Autopilot takes, even on a user's behalf, are tied to its own agent ID and safety-checked, and Autopilot is visible and manageable by admins. Autopilot is rolling out through the Frontier program, Microsoft's early-access route, and it will appear in the left navigation of the Copilot app. Teams will soon be able to bring their personal Autopilot into Workspaces, so humans and agents collaborate in one space.

The intelligence layer is Microsoft IQ and Foundry. Microsoft IQ is the enterprise context management platform that helps organizations build their own IQ by capturing, governing, and reusing the business knowledge that apps and agents need. New business plugins let teams package that knowledge into reusable, trusted context, skills, and tools shared across the business. In Microsoft Foundry, new continuous improvement tools help customers develop more powerful agents and train models automatically based on real production signals, optimizing for quality, latency, and cost. Microsoft-Decision-1.1, a fast, low-cost decision model for routing, classification, prioritization, verification, and workflow control, is now generally available. Microsoft is also connecting Copilot to the systems teams already use: Salesforce in Copilot is in preview with more than 38 pre-built sales skills, and Dynamics 365 Sales and ERP plugins are now generally available.

Microsoft announced its trust and governance capabilities alongside its agents, with availability ranging from generally available security products to Frontier releases, previews, and planned enhancements. Microsoft Perception, now generally available, brings together specialized agents to find, defend, and remediate vulnerabilities, powered by models including Microsoft's MAI-Cyber line. Agent 365 Business Analytics, now available in the Frontier program, gives business leaders data on AI spend, task success, user satisfaction, and assisted hours, so they can see what AI costs and what it delivers. Insights for Microsoft services require at least one Microsoft 365 Copilot license in the tenant; insights for non-Microsoft services require an Agent 365 license. Agent 365 Cost Management, in preview, is a centralized place to see and control usage-based AI services, set spending policies, limits, and alerts, and extend analysis through Microsoft Fabric for chargeback and reporting. Microsoft says spending forecasts and optimization recommendations are available through the Frontier program in November 2026, while an expansion covering value insights across Microsoft and non-Microsoft agents and delegation to business leaders is planned for December. Windows is expanding operating-system-level protection for agents, with Microsoft Execution Containers, built-in agent identity with Entra support, and Agent 365 policies enforced on every device. BNY, building on Microsoft Defender, Entra, and Purview, is exploring capabilities in Project Perception, and, according to Microsoft, BNY's collaboration completed the first agentic negotiation of a US Treasury trade.

The infrastructure underneath is Azure Cobalt 200 virtual machines, now generally available, delivering up to 50 percent better performance than the previous generation, according to Microsoft. One discrepancy to note: Microsoft's global blog says Cobalt 200 will reach 23 Azure regions by year end, while the Book of News says 24 regions by the end of the year. The performance claim matches on both pages; the region count does not, and this draft uses neither number as settled. Azure Catalyst, a unified experience for agentic modernization across Azure and GitHub, is generally available. Customer scale in the keynote: Volkswagen Group is equipping more than 330,000 employees with Copilot, and The Estée Lauder Companies has expanded Copilot to more than 14,000 people. Publicis Groupe is empowering employees to build custom AI skills that combine Microsoft IQ with its industry expertise and proprietary data.

Five checks worth keeping before an organization adopts the Ignite announcements

  1. Check the availability label before planning a rollout. Autopilot, Workspaces, and Copilot Code are in the Frontier program. Agent 365 Cost Management is in preview. Copilot Managed Runtime, Office in Copilot, Microsoft Perception, Cobalt 200, and Azure Catalyst are generally available. A plan built on a Frontier feature is a plan built on early access.
  2. Give the agent an identity you can audit. Autopilot acts under its own agent ID, and its actions are safety-checked and visible to admins. Ask for the log of what it did, who it did it for, and what it touched.
  3. Price the agent before it runs. Agent 365 Cost Management sets spending policies, limits, and alerts across usage-based AI services. Set them before the services pile up, not after the first bill.
  4. Decide who owns the context. Microsoft IQ packages company knowledge into reusable context, skills, and tools. Decide who approves what goes into that package, and who can change it.
  5. Keep a person in the approval path for money and commitments. BNY's agentic Treasury trade shows agents can negotiate. Decide in advance which actions need a person, and write it down before the agent is given the job.

What to watch

whether Copilot Code reaches general availability in December as planned; whether Autopilot widens beyond the Frontier program; whether Agent 365 Cost Management's December expansion to non-Microsoft agents ships; what Agent 365 licensing costs once the analytics move past Frontier; and whether Microsoft corrects the Cobalt 200 region count. The pattern at Ignite was consistent: the agent got a job, an identity, a budget, and a log, in the same announcement. That is the standard to hold every agent platform to.
